    Method and caveats

    Valuations. Each stock's intrinsic value and solvency score come from AlphaSpread, which blends discounted cash flow and relative (multiples) valuation. The valuation gap is how far the price sits below (undervalued, positive) or above (overvalued, negative) that value, measured against the larger of the two: "undervalued by 35%" means the price is 65% of the value, "overvalued by 28%" means the value is 72% of the price. It is one model's estimate, not a price target.

    "Fairly valued" stocks. When the price sits within the range of AlphaSpread's scenarios it labels the stock "Fairly Valued" and prints no percentage. For those the gap is computed the way AlphaSpread computes it for every other stock, from its own base-case value and the market price: the difference divided by the larger of the two. These gaps are small by construction.

    Constituents and weights. The index members, their GICS sector, country and weight come from the holdings of the iShares STOXX Europe 600 UCITS ETF (DE), which fully replicates the index. Its weights are free-float market-cap weights, the same weighting STOXX uses, so "market-cap weighted" here means index-weighted.

    Currencies. Prices and intrinsic values are in each stock's trading currency (EUR, GBP, CHF, SEK, DKK, NOK, PLN). Gaps are percentages, so they compare across currencies.

    Europe vs US. The S&P 500 figures use the AlphaSpread snapshot of the same day (or the nearest earlier one) and that page's market-cap weights. Stocks AlphaSpread doesn't cover are left out of both.
