What is PVGO?

A stock's price has two parts: what the company is worth if it never grew again, and what investors pay for expected future growth. The second part is the Present Value of Growth Opportunities (PVGO).

No-Growth Value = Forward EPS ÷ Cost of Equity

What you would pay for a bond-like company that pays out all its earnings forever, with no reinvestment.

PVGO = Current Price − No-Growth Value

The remainder is what the market pays purely on the belief that the company will find profitable ways to reinvest and grow. PVGO % shows how much of the price is a growth bet:

  • High PVGO % (70–90%): most of the price is growth expectations, so the stock is sensitive to whether they are met.
  • Low or negative PVGO %: the stock trades near or below its no-growth earnings value. The market sees little growth ahead, or the stock may be undervalued.

Cost of Equity uses CAPM: Risk-Free Rate + Beta × Market Risk Premium. The risk-free rate is the 10-year US Treasury yield (^TNX on Yahoo Finance).
