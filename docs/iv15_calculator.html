What is IV15?

IV15 is an intrinsic value estimate built on one disciplined assumption: a business must compound its free cash flow at 15% per year for 15 years and be worth 15× its final-year cash flow at exit, all discounted back at a 15% hurdle rate. If the stock trades below this value, it clears the bar; above it, you are paying a premium for growth that may never materialise.

IV15 = Σ FCFt / (1.15)t + TerminalValue / (1.15)15

The Tragic Algebra: why SBC matters

Companies report Free Cash Flow without deducting Stock-Based Compensation (SBC). SBC is a real cost to shareholders because it dilutes ownership, but as a non-cash expense it disappears from the cash flow statement. The Tragic Algebra corrects this:

True FCF = Reported FCF − Stock-Based Compensation

A company with $10B of FCF but $4B of annual SBC has a True FCF of only $6B. Valuing it on the reported figure overstates intrinsic value by about 67%.

Manual SBC override: Yahoo Finance sometimes omits SBC or reports it with the wrong sign. If results look off, take the SBC figure from the latest 10-K (cash flow statement, operating activities) and enter it in millions.

How to read Price / IV15

  • < 1.0: the stock trades below intrinsic value at a 15% growth assumption. Potential margin of safety.
  • 1.0 – 1.5: a modest premium; growth expectations are priced in but not extreme.
  • > 1.5: a significant premium; the market is pricing in growth well above 15% per year.

IV15 is not a price target but a hurdle test. A ratio above 1 doesn't make the stock a bad investment; it means the implied return at today's price is below 15% a year under these assumptions. Adjust the growth rate and terminal multiple to stress-test your thesis.
